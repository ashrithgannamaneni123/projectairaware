The problem

Bad air, young lungs, one valley.

Our county's asthma rate runs well above California's. Our air basin ranks among the worst in the country. And more of us are children than almost anywhere else in the state.

Where these numbers come from

Why county data instead of Mountain House data?

Because Mountain House data doesn't exist yet. These are the most recent county-level figures the California Department of Public Health has published. Our city only incorporated in 2024, and nobody has measured asthma here on its own — which is a large part of why our survey matters.

Sources for the air quality figures

Pollution-burden rankings, PM2.5 and ozone standings, and the heavy-duty vehicle emissions comparison describe the San Joaquin Valley air basin and San Joaquin County. The characterization of the region's air quality as among the nation's worst comes from the EPA.

Our solutions

A box fan, four filters, and a before-and-after.

Three phases. Each one leaves something behind: a measurement, a working air cleaner in someone's living room, or a lesson a kid remembers.

The kit — tap a part

The idea

One fan, four filters

EPA testing found this kind of build can outperform some considerably more expensive commercial air purifiers. Tap a part of the diagram to see what each piece does.

The part people skip

Filters have to be changed. EPA testing found units running on dirty filters were "almost completely ineffective" — a clogged kit isn't a weaker air cleaner, it's close to none at all. If you get a kit from us, we'll show you what a spent filter looks like.

Source: U.S. EPA research on do-it-yourself air cleaners.

What HOSA is

HOSA – Future Health Professionals is a national student organization for people heading into health care. We compete in its Community Awareness event, which asks a chapter to find a real health problem in its own community and run an evidence-based campaign about it — not write about one.

That shaped everything here: a local issue, local data, a survey run before and after so the result is measured rather than asserted.

Our mission

Reduce asthma risk in Mountain House by making environmental triggers visible, handing people practical tools to cut their exposure, and building prevention habits that stick — grounded in real data.

We're not trying to cover everything about asthma. No emergency training, no clinical content. We make the air here legible to the people breathing it, and put working filters in as many homes as we can reach.

Recognizing symptoms

If someone is having trouble breathing

Common asthma symptoms are wheezing, coughing, shortness of breath, and chest tightness. A person with asthma should use their own prescribed inhaler as their doctor directed. Call 911 if symptoms are severe or don't improve.

That's as far as this page goes. We're a student awareness project, not a medical resource.
